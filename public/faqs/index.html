Under what circumstances can Tohonesty help?

Tohonesty primarily coordinates high-value asset recovery for institutional bodies and high-net-worth individuals. Our referral network facilitates access to specialists in situations involving:

  • Accidental Loss: Scenarios involving lost private keys, corrupted seed phrases, forgotten passphrases, damaged hardware wallets, or missing wallet configuration files.
  • Legal Seizure or Forfeiture: Situations where an institution has secured legal possession of digital assets but lacks the technical keys required for access and liquidation.
  • Unauthorised Misappropriation: Instances where assets have been displaced through infrastructure exploits, compromised network security, or external vulnerabilities.
  • Other Forms of Displaced Access: Any scenario where legal ownership can be independently verified but technical control of the asset has been lost.

Please note that we generally restrict our focus to high-value assets valued at $1 million USD and above. Every case is evaluated on its individual merits, balancing the technical nature of the loss against the legal viability of asset restoration.

What is Tohonesty’s Minimal Access Protocol?

Tohonesty has developed its own Minimal Access Protocol to put data minimisation into practice from the very first contact.

We operate a strict Minimal-Knowledge infrastructure posture. No plain-text data concerning your loss is ever processed, tracked, or cached on our public web servers, hosting platforms, or intermediate network relays.

Data minimisation is a core principle of UK GDPR: organisations should collect only what is necessary, keep it only for as long as needed, and limit who can see it. Our Protocol turns that principle into a concrete operating standard for every case enquiry we receive.

Under the Minimal Access Protocol:

  • We do not maintain a client database.
  • The secure Case Intake Form collects only three pieces of information: your name, email address, and a simple indication of ownership status.
  • We never request or accept private keys, seed phrases, recovery codes or other high-risk technical details.
  • Submissions are received as restricted notifications and are reviewed only by a single authorised and vetted person on a strict need-to-know basis.

How long will it take and how much will it cost to recover my digital property?

There is no fixed timeline or cost because every case is unique. The path to recovery depends almost entirely on:

  • Your evidence: How clearly you can link your identity to the stolen assets.
  • Jurisdiction: Which countries and court systems are involved.
  • Complexity: How the assets were moved or hidden since you lost possession.
  • Cooperation: The extent to which third parties comply with legal requests.

Timeline

Cases with clear, undeniable proof of ownership move much faster. If your assets are scattered across multiple jurisdictions, the process naturally takes longer.

Cost

Costs are driven entirely by the specialised legal and forensic work required. Full cross-border asset recovery actions are serious legal operations and require a committed budget.

The Bottom Line

The single biggest factor that cuts down both time and cost is the quality of evidence you hold. The clearer the link between your identity and the assets, the more efficient the recovery process will be. We cannot give you a reliable estimate until our network of specialists reviews the specific facts of your case.

What is Tohonesty's legal philosophy?

Tohonesty operates on a straightforward legal principle: digital assets are private property, subject to the exact same foundational legal standards as traditional assets like corporate shares, real estate, or vehicles.

Our core methodology rests on three key legal realities:

  • Title Overrides Technical Control: A private key provides functional utility on a blockchain, but it does not dictate ownership. Legal title depends entirely on your ability to link your identity to the asset history. Losing access to a key does not mean you forfeit your legal rights to the property.
  • Digital Assets Fall Under Established Law: Digital assets are not a lawless exception. High courts globally, including the English High Court, have long established that digital assets are personal property protected by common law.
  • The “Private Key Myth” is Legally Flawed: The popular phrase “not your keys, not your coin” is a technical observation, not a legal truth. It incorrectly confuses temporary technical possession with permanent legal ownership.

Because ownership is a question of law rather than software code, asset recovery does not rely on holding the original private keys. Where ownership can be sufficiently documented, legal remedies exist to restore access. Tohonesty’s role is to evaluate case viability and connect you with elite specialist partners equipped to execute ownership-based recovery.

What is the difference between ownership of digital assets and possession of keys?

Possession of a private key represents technical control, not legal ownership. A private key simply grants the functional ability to move an asset across a blockchain network. True legal ownership is a separate property right determined by law, not by whoever happens to hold the cryptographic access strings.

A clear analogy illustrates this principle: if you lose your car keys—or if someone else takes possession of them—your legal title to the vehicle remains unchanged. The law recognises your ownership and can issue orders to restore access or recover the property.

The same framework applies to digital assets, which are legally recognised as private property. The legal system, rather than software code, holds the ultimate authority to determine ownership.

If you are told that asset recovery is impossible without a private key, that advice reflects a misunderstanding of property law. Tohonesty’s model focuses entirely on verifying legal ownership first. Restoring technical control or access is simply the execution phase of that legal right.
