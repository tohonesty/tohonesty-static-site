What service does Tohonesty provide?

Tohonesty is an independent introduction and referral service for high-net-worth individuals and institutional entities seeking appropriately qualified legal and forensic assistance with high-value digital-asset matters.

  • Initial Suitability Review: We consider the limited information submitted through our contact form against our referral criteria. This is not a legal opinion or forensic assessment.
  • Authorised Introduction: If an enquiry appears suitable, and only with your express consent, we may introduce you to an independent specialist.
  • Independent Assessment: Any legal or forensic evaluation is undertaken by the specialist under its own professional responsibilities and engagement terms.
  • Your Decision: The specialist explains its proposed scope, fees, risks and possible next steps directly to you. You decide independently whether to engage it.

Tohonesty is not a law firm, recovery contractor, financial adviser, forensic laboratory or asset custodian. We do not investigate claims, conduct litigation or take control of digital assets.

Under what circumstances can Tohonesty help?

Tohonesty may be able to make an introduction where an institutional body or high-net-worth individual requires specialist advice concerning a high-value digital-asset matter, including:

  • Accidental Loss: Scenarios involving lost private keys, corrupted seed phrases, forgotten passphrases, damaged hardware wallets, or missing wallet configuration files.
  • Legal Seizure or Forfeiture: Situations in which an institution has obtained a seizure, confiscation or forfeiture order but practical control or realisation remains unresolved.
  • Unauthorised Misappropriation: Suspected fraud, theft, infrastructure compromise or unauthorised transfer requiring legal and forensic assessment.
  • Other Forms of Displaced Access: Situations in which an asserted ownership interest can be supported by evidence but technical control has been lost or is disputed.

We generally restrict our focus to matters valued at USD 1 million or more. Every enquiry is considered individually. Meeting the value threshold does not establish ownership, legal viability, technical recoverability or acceptance by Tohonesty or any specialist.

Neither Tohonesty nor any person acting for it will attempt to bypass wallet security, derive unknown private keys, exploit a protocol or obtain unauthorised access.

What is your minimum threshold for taking on a case?

Tohonesty focuses on higher-value digital asset recovery. Because cross-border legal tracing and restitution pathways require significant resources, we typically only consider cases involving a displacement or loss of $1 million USD (or the equivalent in other currencies) or more.

Matters below this threshold may be disproportionate to the potential cost of specialist legal and forensic work, although that assessment is case-specific.

Meeting the threshold does not guarantee a referral, acceptance by a specialist or recovery. Relevant considerations may include the evidence, type and location of the assets, applicable law, jurisdiction, available respondents or intermediaries, technical circumstances, likely cost and proportionality.

What is Tohonesty’s Minimal Access Protocol?

Tohonesty has developed its own Minimal Access Protocol to put data minimisation into practice from the very first contact.

The public website is designed so that plaintext form contents are encrypted in the user's browser before the network submission occurs. The contact endpoint receives an encrypted envelope. The initial form is deliberately unsuitable for providing a case narrative, evidence or security credentials.

Data minimisation is a principle of UK data-protection law: personal data should be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the stated purpose. Our Protocol applies that principle to the initial enquiry process.

Under the Minimal Access Protocol:

  • We do not use the public website to build a case file or evidence repository.
  • The secure contact form is limited to your name, email address, an indication of ownership or evidentiary status, and confirmation of consent.
  • We never request or accept private keys, seed phrases, passwords, PINs, passphrases, recovery codes or direct wallet access.
  • Access to decrypted submissions is restricted to an authorised person on a need-to-know basis.

How long will it take and how much will it cost to recover my digital property?

There is no reliable general timeline or cost. A specialist would need to examine the facts before deciding whether any pathway is available or proportionate. Relevant factors may include:

  • Evidence: Whether ownership or another enforceable interest, relevant events and the asset trail can be proved with admissible evidence.
  • Jurisdiction: Which countries and court systems are involved.
  • Asset and tracing complexity: The type of asset, custody arrangement, transactions, substitutions, mixing and dissipation.
  • Available respondents: Whether an identifiable and reachable person, issuer, exchange, custodian or other intermediary has relevant information, assets or control.
  • Timing and proportionality: Whether useful preservation steps remain available and whether the likely benefit justifies the cost and risk.

Timeline

Urgent preservation or disclosure applications may move quickly, while investigation, litigation, recognition and enforcement can take substantially longer. Strong evidence may improve efficiency but does not guarantee a short process or successful outcome.

Cost

Costs depend on the legal and forensic work, jurisdictions, counterparties, applications, experts, disclosure, litigation, enforcement and funding arrangements required. Costs can be substantial and may not be recoverable from another party even if a claim succeeds.

The Bottom Line

Evidence quality is important, but it is only one part of the assessment. Legal entitlement, technical control, traceability, jurisdiction, available respondents, timing and economics must be considered separately. Tohonesty cannot estimate the duration, cost or prospects of a matter; an independently engaged specialist may provide an assessment after reviewing sufficient information.

What is Tohonesty's legal philosophy?

Tohonesty's approach begins with a careful distinction: legal entitlement, technical control and practical recovery are related, but they are not the same thing.

Under English law, the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 confirms that a thing is not prevented from being an object of personal property rights merely because it is neither a traditional thing in possession nor a thing in action. The Act does not declare that every digital asset is property, prescribe identical rights for every asset or guarantee a remedy.

Our approach reflects three principles:

  • Control is not conclusive ownership: A private key usually provides the practical ability to authorise transactions. That fact may be important evidence, but legal entitlement depends on the asset, transactions, contracts, custody arrangements and applicable law.
  • Property status is the starting point: Where an asset is capable of being property, proprietary remedies, tracing and protective orders may be available. The claimant must still establish an enforceable interest, an appropriate cause of action, jurisdiction and the factual basis for relief.
  • An order is not the same as recovery: A court can determine rights and impose obligations on persons within its effective reach. It cannot necessarily recreate a lost key, reverse a decentralised ledger or produce recoverable value where assets have been dissipated.

For that reason, a responsible assessment separates three questions: can a legal right be proved, can an effective order or agreement be obtained, and can that result be converted into practical control or value? Tohonesty does not answer those legal questions; where appropriate, we introduce prospective clients to independent specialists who can assess them.

What is the difference between ownership of digital assets and possession of keys?

A private key generally provides the technical ability to authorise a transaction. It does not necessarily prove that the key holder is legally entitled to the asset: a key may be held by a custodian, trustee, employee, thief or other person acting for or against the owner.

Conversely, a person may retain or obtain a legal interest while lacking the key needed to exercise control. Whether that interest amounts to ownership, a contractual claim, a trust interest or another right depends on the relevant asset, acquisition, custody terms, evidence and governing law.

The distinction matters because a judgment or forfeiture order may establish entitlement without changing the blockchain state. If no usable key exists, no cooperative custodian or issuer has control, and no person can be compelled to provide effective access, the asset may remain technically immovable despite the order.

It is therefore too broad to say either that keys always determine ownership or that legal ownership always enables recovery. A specialist must examine legal entitlement and practical enforceability separately.
