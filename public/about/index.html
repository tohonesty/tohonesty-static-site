Dismantling the Private Key Myth

A pervasive misconception within the digital asset ecosystem is the maxim of “not your keys, not your coin.” This technical fallacy has led corporations and asset managers to mistakenly assume that a loss of cryptographic access equates to a loss of legal title. Under prevailing legal frameworks, including UK property law, digital assets are recognized as personal property. Proprietary rights are inextricably tied to legal identity, not to an isolated string of cryptographic code.

Possession vs. Legal Ownership

To secure institutional restitution, we operate under three immutable legal principles:

  • Access is Not Title: A private key grants the technical utility to execute transfers, but it does not establish or transfer legal title.
  • Theft Does Not Transfer Right: Fraudulent or unauthorized transfers do not always grant legal ownership to the recipient. The assets – or their traceable substitutes—could remain classified as tainted and subject to proprietary restitution (BFP).
  • Identity Establishes Superior Claim: When corporate or individual identity can be verified and linked to the source of funds, legal ownership remains enforceable under court jurisdiction.
